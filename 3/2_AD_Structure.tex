\section{Additive Difference Structure} \label{sec-AD-ps}
% In diesem Abschnitt geht es darum, wann eine factorial proximity structure durch das additive difference model (siehe \ref{add-diff-model}) dargestellt werden kann. Die Axiome die dafür benötigt sind, werden hier nicht genauer dargestellt (siehe Anhang \ref{appendix}), sonder nur genannt.
In this section we will develop necessary and sufficient conditions for a proximity structure to be representable by an AD Model.
We do this by examining each of the characteristics of the AD model, namely decomposability, intradimensional subtractivity and interdimensional additivity, and deriving conditions for a representation. The conditions we derive are closely related to the theory developed in \textcite{foundations1} and most of the theorems utilize some sort of reduction to the structures developed there.
At the end of this subsection, we will state the representation and uniqueness theorem.
We begin with decomposability because it is the basis assumption of each of the variants.

Decomposability implies that we can represent the observed proximity of two stimuli pairs by a function that evaluates the proximity on each factor.
By monotonicity in each argument, for two stimuli pairs that only differ on the $i$-th dimension, the ordering must be preserved when the values on the remaining factors are changed in the same way.
This means that each factor contributes its effect to the overall similarity independently of all the other factors. In particular, This is stated in the next definition, where $(\ps{a}, \ps{b})$ and $(\ps{a'}, \ps{b'})$ are the original stimuli and $\ps{c}, \ps{d}, \ps{c'}$ and $\ps{d'}$ are the stimuli we obtain by ``changing'' the remaining dimensions, as just described.

\begin{ax}[One-Factor Independence] \label{def-onef-indep}
A factorial proximity structure $\langle A, \succsim \rangle$ satisfies \emph{one-factor independence} iff the following holds for all $\ps{a}, \ps{a'}, \ps{b}, \ps{b'}, \ps{c}, \ps{c'}, \ps{d}, \ps{d'} \in A$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item If the two elements in each of the pairs $(\ps{a}, \ps{a'})$, $(\ps{b}, \ps{b'})$, $(\ps{c}, \ps{c'})$, $(\ps{d}, \ps{d'})$ have identical components on all but one factor, and 
  \item the two elements in each of the pairs $(\ps{a}, \ps{c})$, $(\ps{a'}, \ps{c'})$, $(\ps{b}, \ps{d})$, $(\ps{b'}, \ps{d'})$ have identical components on the remaining factor, then  
\end{enumerate}
$$
(\ps{a}, \ps{b}) \succsim (\ps{a'}, \ps{b'}) \quad \text{iff} \quad (\ps{c}, \ps{d}) \succsim (\ps{c'}, \ps{d'}).
$$
\end{ax}

One-factor independence induces an ordering on each factor and asserts that the induced ordering is well-defined, i.e., independent of the fixed components on the remaining factors.
This induced ordering is denoted by $\succsim_i$. This ordering is of importance for developing intradimensional subtractivity, which we do next.

Intradimensional subtractivity is obtained by establishing decomposability for the entire structure and then imposing an absolute-difference structure from \textcite[pp. 170-173, Definition 4.8]{foundations1} on each factor $\langle \ps{A}_i^2, \succsim_i \rangle$. Therefore, the axioms for intradimensional subtractivity are essentially the same as for absolute-difference structures, but reformulated in terms of $\langle \ps{A}_i^2, \succsim_i \rangle$ for each $i=1, \dots, n$.\\
Since absolute-difference structures and its axioms are unidimensional, the comparison of two pairs can be seen as the comparison of two intervals, and therefore the ordering can be seen as an ordering of intervals. This introduces the intuitive notion of betweenness on the real number line, but reformulated in terms of the unidimensional ordering relation.
By one-factor independence, we get that these concepts must apply for each factor of the proximity structure, and therefore are necessary conditions.
Next, we present the definitions of betweenness and the betweenness axioms. The first betweenness axiom can easily be verified using 4 points on the number line. For a visualization of the second axiom, see \Cref{fig-between}.

\begin{ax}[Betweenness and Betweenness Axioms] \label{def-between-ax}
Let $\langle A = \prod_{i=1}^n A_i, \succsim \rangle$ be a factorial proximity structure that satisfies one-factor independence. Let $\succsim_i$ denote the induced order on $A^2_i$. We say that $\ps{b}$ is \emph{between} $\ps{a}$ and $\ps{c}$, denoted $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{c}$, if and only if
\[
  (\ps{a}_i, \ps{c}_i) \succsim_i (\ps{a}_i, \ps{b}_i), (\ps{b}_i, \ps{c}_i) \quad \text{for each } i.
\]
We say that $\langle A, \succsim \rangle$ satisfies the \emph{betweenness axioms} iff the following hold for all $\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{d}, \ps{a'}, \ps{b'}, \ps{c'} \in A$:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Suppose $\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{d}$ differ on at most one factor, and $\ps{b} \neq \ps{c}$, then
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item[(i)] if $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{c}$ and $\ps{b}|\ps{c}|\ps{d}$, then $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{d}$ and $\ps{a}|\ps{c}|\ps{d}$;
        \item[(ii)] if $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{c}$ and $\ps{a}|\ps{c}|\ps{d}$, then $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{d}$ and $\ps{b}|\ps{c}|\ps{d}$.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Suppose $\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{a'}, \ps{b'}, \ps{c'}$ differ on at most one factor, $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{c}$, $\ps{a'}|\ps{b'}|\ps{c'}$, and $(\ps{b}, \ps{c}) \sim (\ps{b'}, \ps{c'})$, then
    \[
    (\ps{a}, \ps{c}) \succsim (\ps{a'}, \ps{c'}) \quad  \text{iff} \quad  (\ps{a}, \ps{b}) \succsim (\ps{a'}, \ps{b'}).
    \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{ax}

The betweenness axioms are, however, not enough to establish an absolute-difference structure on each factor. We further need two technical axioms, which are not empirically testable.\\
The first one is restricted solvability which asserts that, given a range of dissimilarity defined by a starting point and two end points, we can construct a second point \emph{between the two end points} which attains that dissimilarity.
Formulated in terms of a proximity structure this yields the following axiom, where $\ps{d}$ is the starting point, $(\ps{d}, \ps{a})$ and $(\ps{d}, \ps{c})$ define the range of dissimilarity and $\ps{b}$ is the point, that realizes this dissimilarity and is between $\ps{a}$ and $\ps{c}$.

\begin{ax}[Restricted Solvability] \label{defi-restrict-solv}
A factorial proximity structure $\langle A, \succsim \rangle$ satisfies \emph{restricted solvability} iff, for all elements $\ps{e}, \ps{f}, \ps{d}, \ps{a}, \ps{c} \in A$, if $(\ps{d}, \ps{a}) \succsim (\ps{e}, \ps{f}) \succsim (\ps{d}, \ps{c})$, then there exists $\ps{b} \in A$ such that $\ps{a}|\ps{b}|\ps{c}$ and $(\ps{d}, \ps{b}) \sim (\ps{e}, \ps{f})$.
\end{ax}

The second axiom is the Archimedean axiom, which is a basic axiom in most representation measurement structures.
The statement of this axiom can be formulated as follows: Given a distance, a radius, and a center, every sequence of points starting from the center and moving away from the center with steps greater than the given distance exits the given radius in a finite amount of steps.
In other words, for such a sequence either the steps get smaller or the sequence moves out of the radius.
This can be formulated with respect to $\succsim$ as follows.

\begin{ax}[Archimedean axiom] \label{def-archimed-ax-adm}
A proximity structure $\langle A, \succsim \rangle$ satisfies the \emph{Archimedean axiom} if and only if, for all $\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{d} \in A$ with $\ps{a} \neq \ps{b}$, any sequence $\ps{e}^{(k)} \in A$, $k = 0, 1, \ldots$ such that $\ps{e}^{(0)} = \ps{c}$, $(\ps{e}^{(k)}, \ps{e}^{(k+1)}) \succ (\ps{a}, \ps{b})$, and $(\ps{c}, \ps{d}) \succ (\ps{c}, \ps{e}^{(k+1)}) \succ (\ps{c}, \ps{e}^{(k)})$ for all $k$ is finite.
\end{ax}

Given these axioms, we can establish an absolute-difference structure on each factor and therefore get an intradimensionally subtractive representation \parencite[p. 181]{foundations2}.

Finally, we investigate some consequences of interdimensional additivity.
Let $\ps{a}$ and $\ps{a}'$ be two stimuli that coincide on the $i$-th factor, and let the same hold for $\ps{b}$ and $\ps{b}'$. Then $\psi_i(\ps{a}_i, \ps{b}_i) = \psi_i(\ps{a}'_i, \ps{b}'_i)$ and, since our representation is additive, the relation $(\ps{a}, \ps{b}) \succsim (\ps{a}', \ps{b}')$ must be independent of the $i$-th component.
Moreover, changing the common value of $\ps{a}$ and $\ps{a}'$ and the common value of $\ps{b}$ and $\ps{b}'$ should preserve the ordering.
Therefore, the ordering is independent of the common value.
This independence is different from one-factor independence which we defined above and is, in fact, stronger, as, in the presence of the other axioms, it implies one-factor independence, but not conversely \parencite[p. 184]{foundations2}.

\begin{ax}[Independence] \label{def-independ}
A factorial proximity structure $\langle A, \succsim \rangle$ satisfies \emph{independence} if the following holds for all $\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{d}, \ps{a'}, \ps{b'}, \ps{c'}, \ps{d'} \in A$:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item If the two elements in each of the pairs $(\ps{a}, \ps{a'}), (\ps{b}, \ps{b'}), (\ps{c}, \ps{c'})$ and $(\ps{d}, \ps{d'})$ have identical components on one factor, and
  \item the two elements in each of the pairs $(\ps{a}, \ps{c})$, $(\ps{b}, \ps{d})$, $(\ps{a'}, \ps{c'})$ and $(\ps{b'}, \ps{d'})$ have identical elements on the remaining factors, then
\end{enumerate}
$$
(\ps{c}, \ps{d}) \succsim (\ps{c'}, \ps{d'}) \quad \text{ iff } \quad  (\ps{a}, \ps{b}) \succsim (\ps{a'}, \ps{b'}).
$$
\end{ax}

Proximity structures that satisfy the axiom just defined are called additive difference structures, as formalized in the next definition.
The theorem after that shows, that additive difference structures can be represented by the AD model.
Note, that one-factor independence is implied by independence and is therefore omitted.

\begin{defi}[Additive-Difference Structure]\label{def-add-diff-ps} \index{Additive-difference!structure}
  A factorial proximity structure $\langle \ps{A}, \succsim \rangle$ with $n \geq 3$ factors is an \emph{additive-difference structure} (AD Structure), iff the following axioms hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Independence
  \item Betweenness axioms
  \item Restricted solvability
  \item Archimedean axiom
\end{enumerate}
\end{defi}


\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Representation and Uniqueness Theorem for AD Structures}

\begin{thm}[Representation and Uniqueness Theorem for AD Structures]\label{thm-repr-add-diff-struct} \index{Theorem!representation and uniqueness!for additive-difference structures}
  Let \(\langle \ps{A}, \succsim \rangle\) be an additive-difference structure.  
  Then there exist real-valued functions \(\varphi_1, \ldots, \varphi_n\) defined on \(\ps{A}_1, \ldots, \ps{A}_n\), and increasing functions \(F_1, \ldots, F_n\) each defined on \(\mathbb{R}\), such that for all \(\ps{a}, \ps{b}, \ps{c}, \ps{d} \in \ps{A}\),  
\begin{align*}
  (\ps{a}_1, \ldots, \ps{a}_n, \ps{b}_1, \ldots, \ps{b}_n) &\succsim (\ps{c}_1, \ldots, \ps{c}_n, \ps{d}_1, \ldots, \ps{d}_n) \\
                                                           &\text{ iff } \\
  \sum_{i=1}^{n} F_i(|\varphi_i(\ps{a}_i) - \varphi_i(\ps{b}_i)|) &\geq \sum_{i=1}^{n} F_i(|\varphi_i(\ps{c}_i) - \varphi_i(\ps{d}_i)|).
\end{align*}
Furthermore, the \(\varphi_i\) are interval scales, and the \(F_i\) are interval scales with a common unit (\(i = 1, \ldots, n\)).
\end{thm}



\begin{rem}
  For $n=2$ another axiom, the Thomsen condition, must be assumed. For $n \geq 3$ this conditions follows from the other axioms, see \textcite[pp. 182-184]{foundations2}.
\end{rem}
