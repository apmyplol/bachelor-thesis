
\setglossarypreamble{
Throughout this thesis, we follow a consistent notation and try to use conventional notations and notations introduced in the used literature. Here we will provide a quick overview over the notation.

$\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ denote the real and rational numbers, respectively, with restrictions indicated via subscripts (e.g. $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$).
Other sets are generally denoted with uppercase letters, except for the letters $F$ and $G$, which are real-valued functions from the AD model and are used throughout this thesis.\\
Dimensions are represented by $n$ and $i, k$ and $m$ are integers used for indexing or counting.\\
Vectors are bold (e.g. $\ve{x}, \ve{y}$) with coordinates $\ve{x} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$. Sequences of vectors are indexed as $\ve{x_i}$ or $\ve{x^{(i)}}$.\\
Greek letters are either real-valued functions and have a special meaning (such as $\varphi, \delta$ or $\gamma$) or constants defined in the context (e.g. $\alpha, \beta$).
Other symbols are used according to conventions, e.g. $\lambda$ for convex combinations, $\varepsilon$ for small real quantities and $|x|$ for the absolute value of $x$.


% sets 
Any symbols not mentioned here will be introduced as needed within the relevant sections.
}
